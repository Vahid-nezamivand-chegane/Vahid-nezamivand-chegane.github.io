Energy Intelligence

Geothermal vs. Intermittent Renewable Energy

Solar, wind and hydropower are important low-carbon technologies. Their output profiles differ from geothermal generation, which can provide firm power when the reservoir and plant are properly designed and maintained.

TechnologyStrengthOperating consideration
SolarLow-carbon electricity during daylightOutput varies with daylight, clouds and season; storage or balancing may be needed.
WindLarge-scale renewable generationOutput varies with wind speed and weather conditions.
HydropowerDispatchable generation and potential storageDepends on water availability, reservoir conditions and environmental constraints.
GeothermalPotential firm 24/7 baseload electricityDepends on reservoir performance, well design, maintenance and plant availability.
FIRM POWER POTENTIAL

24 Hours a Day. 7 Days a Week.

Unlike weather-dependent generation, a properly engineered geothermal plant can operate continuously and supply electricity around the clock. Actual annual availability depends on maintenance, reservoir management and technical performance.

Environmental Restoration

Sand-Locking and Desert Ecosystem Restoration

A physical sand stabilization approach using sand-filled geotextile tubes arranged in connected square cells.

Sand-filled geotextile grid

Geotextile tubes filled with local sand are laid in a connected checkerboard pattern. The raised grid divides moving sand into protected square cells and reduces wind-driven transport.

Moisture and soil recovery

Rainfall or controlled irrigation wets the geotextile and the sand or soil in the cells. The grid can help retain seeds and organic matter. Over time, suitable roots and vegetation may improve soil structure and support ecological recovery.

Long-term restoration potential

The stabilized cells can provide suitable planting spaces for native grasses, shrubs, trees and selected crops, depending on soil salinity, water availability and local ecology. The grid is a physical stabilization system, not a chemical soil binder. Field performance should be documented through monitoring.

Carbon capture and verification

Algae can assimilate CO₂ during growth. Net climate benefits depend on the source of CO₂, cultivation energy, harvesting, processing, product lifetime and final biomass use. Permanent carbon removal or carbon credits require independent measurement, reporting and verification.
